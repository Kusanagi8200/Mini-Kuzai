### Turn 6 - ASSISTANT

<pre>
When a quotation contains quotation marks within it, it&#x27;s called a nested quotation. In American English, the standard practice is to use single quotation marks for the inner quotation and double quotation marks for the outer quotation. Here&#x27;s an example:

The professor said, &quot;The student&#x27;s essay began with the line &#x27;To be or not to be, that is the question&#x27; from Shakespeare&#x27;s Hamlet.&quot;

In this example, the outer quotation is set off with double quotation marks, while the inner quotation is set off with single quotation marks. This helps to distinguish between the two levels of quotation and avoids confusion.

In British English, the convention is often reversed, with single quotation marks used for the outer quotation and double quotation marks for the inner quotation. However, in American English, the double-single quotation mark scheme is more commonly used.
