## Public external source dialogue

Source: https://huggingface.co/datasets/HuggingFaceTB/smol-smoltalk

### Turn 1 - USER

<pre>
I am burnt out with my job I have been working non-stop for two years, and my creativity and passion have dwindled significantly. My manager is very supportive however I am finding hard to tell them how I feel because I fear of letting them down. How can I have this conversation without feeling too nervous?
</pre>

### Turn 2 - ASSISTANT

<pre>
It&#x27;s completely normal to feel burnt out after two years of non-stop work, and it&#x27;s great that you&#x27;re recognizing the need to address this issue. Having an open and honest conversation with your manager can be a great first step towards finding a solution.

Before you have the conversation, take some time to reflect on your feelings and what you hope to achieve. Consider what specifically is causing your burnout - is it the workload, lack of challenge, or something else? What changes would you like to see in your role or work environment that would help you regain your creativity and passion?

When you&#x27;re ready to talk to your manager, try to approach the conversation as a collaborative problem-solving exercise rather than a confession of failure. Emphasize your commitment to your job and your desire to find ways to re-ignite your passion and creativity. You might start by expressing your appreciation for your manager&#x27;s support and then explain how you&#x27;re feeling, using &quot;I&quot; statements to describe your emotions and avoid blame.

For example, you could say something like: &quot;I wanted to talk to you about my workload and how I&#x27;m feeling. I&#x27;ve been working non-stop for two years and I&#x27;m starting to feel burnt out. I love my job and I&#x27;m committed to doing great work, but I&#x27;m finding it hard to maintain my creativity and enthusiasm. I was hoping we could discuss some options for adjusting my workload or finding ways to make my role more engaging.&quot;

Remember that your manager is there to support you, and it&#x27;s in their interest to help you succeed. They may be more understanding and willing to work with you than you think. Take a deep breath, be honest, and see the conversation as an opportunity to work together to find a solution.
</pre>

### Turn 3 - USER

<pre>
How do I explain how the burnout got this far without making myself look incompetent to work?
</pre>

### Turn 4 - ASSISTANT

<pre>
When explaining how the burnout got this far, it&#x27;s essential to focus on the circumstances and the impact of your workload, rather than your personal abilities or competence. You can describe the situation objectively, without apportioning blame or making excuses.

You might say something like: &quot;Over the past two years, I&#x27;ve been taking on increasingly complex projects and responsibilities. While I&#x27;ve been able to deliver results, I&#x27;ve been consistently working at a high intensity without a break. I&#x27;ve been trying to manage my workload, but I&#x27;ve reached a point where I&#x27;m feeling overwhelmed and exhausted. I realize now that I should have spoken up sooner, but I was focused on meeting deadlines and delivering results.&quot;

This approach shows that you&#x27;re taking responsibility for your work and acknowledging the impact of your workload, without implying that you&#x27;re incompetent. You can also highlight any factors that contributed to the burnout, such as lack of resources, insufficient support, or unrealistic expectations.

It&#x27;s also important to emphasize what you&#x27;ve learned from the experience and what you would do differently in the future. This demonstrates that you&#x27;re proactive, self-aware, and committed to growth and improvement.

Remember, burnout is a common experience that can happen to anyone, regardless of their competence or abilities. By framing your burnout as a result of circumstances rather than personal failing, you can have an honest conversation with your manager without damaging your professional reputation.
</pre>

### Turn 5 - USER

<pre>
Can I point to anything external such as the overall atmosphere of the company as potential reason without making my company look bad?
</pre>

### Turn 6 - ASSISTANT

<pre>
Yes, you can discuss the overall atmosphere of the company as a contributing factor to your burnout without making the company look bad. It&#x27;s essential to approach this conversation in a constructive and solution-focused way, rather than simply criticizing the company.

You might say something like: &quot;I think the company&#x27;s fast-paced and dynamic environment, while exciting and motivating, can also be demanding and exhausting at times. I&#x27;ve noticed that many colleagues are working long hours and managing heavy workloads, which can make it challenging to maintain a healthy work-life balance. I&#x27;m not sure if others are feeling the same way, but for me, it&#x27;s started to take a toll on my energy and creativity.&quot;

By framing your comment as a personal observation and focusing on the impact on your own well-being, you can discuss the company atmosphere without making a negative attack on the organization. You can also emphasize your commitment to the company&#x27;s mission and values, and express your desire to find ways to thrive in the environment.

If you want to suggest potential solutions, you could say something like: &quot;I think it would be helpful if we could explore ways to prioritize well-being and self-care within the company. This could include initiatives like flexible work arrangements, employee wellness programs, or simply encouraging open conversations about workload and stress. I believe that by supporting the well-being of employees, we can actually increase productivity and job satisfaction in the long run.&quot;

By focusing on potential solutions and framing your comment as a positive suggestion, you can discuss the company atmosphere in a way that is constructive and supportive, rather than critical or negative.
</pre>
