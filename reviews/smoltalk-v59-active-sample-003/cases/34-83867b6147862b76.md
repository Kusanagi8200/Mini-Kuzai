<pre>
The limit definition of a derivative is given by the expression f&#x27;(x) = lim(h → 0) [f(x + h) - f(x)]/h, where f(x) is the function we want to differentiate and h is an infinitesimally small change in x. To find the derivative of f(x) = 3x^2 + x using this definition, we start by substituting f(x + h) and f(x) into the formula.

Substituting f(x + h) = 3(x + h)^2 + (x + h) and f(x) = 3x^2 + x, we get f&#x27;(x) = lim(h → 0) [3(x + h)^2 + (x + h) - (3x^2 + x)]/h.

Expanding the terms, we have f&#x27;(x) = lim(h → 0) [3(x^2 + 2xh + h^2) + x + h - 3x^2 - x]/h. Simplifying further gives f&#x27;(x) = lim(h → 0) [6xh + 3h^2 + h]/h.

Dividing the numerator and denominator by h (and assuming h is not equal to 0), we can simplify to f&#x27;(x) = lim(h → 0) [6x + 3h + 1]. As h approaches 0, the term 3h vanishes, leaving us with f&#x27;(x) = 6x + 1.

This represents the rate of change of the function f(x) = 3x^2 + x with respect to x at any point x. In other words, the derivative f&#x27;(x) tells us the instantaneous rate at which the value of the function changes when x changes. Geometrically, it can be thought of as the slope of the tangent line to the curve of f(x) at a given point x. This is a fundamental concept in calculus and analysis, with numerous applications in physics, engineering, and other fields where rates of change and slopes of curves play a crucial role.

The product rule states that if we have a function f(x) = u(x)v(x), where u(x) and v(x) are two differentiable functions, then the derivative f&#x27;(x) is given by f&#x27;(x) = u&#x27;(x)v(x) + u(x)v&#x27;(x).

The product rule allows us to elegantly differentiate products of functions, which is essential in a wide range of mathematical and scientific applications.

Define a geometric series, and what is a general formula for the sum of a geometric series that terminates after n terms?
</pre>

### Turn 6 - ASSISTANT

<pre>
A geometric series is a sequence of numbers where each term after the first is found by multiplying the previous term by a fixed, non-zero constant called the common ratio. This means that if the first term is a and the common ratio is r, the series will be: a, ar, ar^2, ar^3, ...

A geometric series can be finite (terminating) or infinite, but here we&#x27;ll focus on a terminating geometric series with n terms. The general form of such a series is: a + ar + ar^2 + ... + ar^(n-1).

The sum of a terminating geometric series with n terms can be calculated using the formula:

S_n = a * (1 - r^n) / (1 - r)

where S_n is the sum of the first n terms, a is the first term, r is the common ratio, and n is the number of terms. This formula is valid as long as r ≠ 1. If r = 1, the series is not geometric, as each term would be the same.

To see why this formula works, consider the following: if we multiply the entire series by r, we get ar + ar^2 + ar^3 + ... + ar^n. Subtracting this from the original series, we get:

(a + ar + ar^2 + ... + ar^(n-1)) - (ar + ar^2 + ar^3 + ... + ar^n) = a - ar^n

Factoring out a and using the formula for the difference of two powers, we can simplify this to a(1 - r^n). The left-hand side of the equation can also be written as (1 - r)(a + ar + ar^2 + ... + ar^(n-1)), which equals (1 - r)S_n. Equating the two expressions and solving for S_n, we arrive at the formula: S_n = a * (1 - r^n) / (1 - r).

This formula has numerous applications in mathematics, finance, and other fields where geometric progressions are used to model real-world phenomena.
</pre>
