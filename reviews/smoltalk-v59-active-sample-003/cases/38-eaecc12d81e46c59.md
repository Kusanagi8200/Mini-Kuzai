How can you describe active listening when working with friends.

### Turn 6 - ASSISTANT

<pre>
Active listening is a powerful way to show your friends that you value and respect them, and it&#x27;s especially important when they&#x27;re sharing their struggles or stress with you. When practicing active listening with friends, you&#x27;re not just hearing their words, but also paying attention to their tone, body language, and emotions.

It&#x27;s about giving your undivided attention to your friend, putting away distractions like your phone or other tasks, and making eye contact. As your friend speaks, focus on fully understanding their perspective, and avoid interrupting or mentally preparing your response. Instead, allow yourself to fully absorb what they&#x27;re saying, and show that you&#x27;re engaged in the conversation.

You can demonstrate active listening by using verbal and non-verbal cues, such as nodding your head, using phrases like &quot;I see&quot; or &quot;I understand,&quot; and summarizing what your friend has said to ensure you grasp their concerns. This helps your friend feel heard and validated, and it also helps you better comprehend their situation.

It&#x27;s also essential to listen without judgment, avoiding the urge to offer unsolicited advice or criticize their thoughts or feelings. Sometimes, friends just need someone to listen to them without trying to fix the problem or offer solutions. By listening actively, you&#x27;re providing a safe and supportive space for your friend to express themselves, which can be incredibly therapeutic and help them feel more at ease.

Remember, active listening is not about being a passive recipient of information; it&#x27;s about being fully present and engaged in the conversation, showing your friend that you care about what they&#x27;re going through, and that you&#x27;re there to support them.
</pre>
