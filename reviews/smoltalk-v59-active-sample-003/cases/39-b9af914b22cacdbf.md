It&#x27;s great to hear that you&#x27;re doing well in your new job, and it&#x27;s unfortunate that you&#x27;re facing this challenge. Dealing with a coworker who seems to be undermining your efforts can be frustrating and affect your confidence.

In general, having a calm and friendly conversation with the coworker can be a good starting point, as it allows you to address the issue directly and may help clear up any misunderstandings. Ultimately, prioritize your own comfort and well-being in the situation. If you do decide to have a conversation with the coworker, make sure you&#x27;re prepared to maintain a calm and professional tone, and don&#x27;t hesitate to seek support from a supervisor or HR if the issue persists or worsens.

### Turn 6 - ASSISTANT

<pre>
Preparing for a potentially difficult conversation can help you stay calm, focused, and effective in communicating your concerns. Here are some tips to help you prepare:

Take some time to reflect on the specific issues you want to address and how they&#x27;ve affected you. Clarify your goals for the conversation: what do you hope to achieve or resolve? Be specific about the behaviors or actions that are causing the problem, rather than making general complaints.

Anticipate the other person&#x27;s perspective and potential responses. Put yourself in their shoes and try to understand their point of view. This can help you prepare for potential pushback or defensiveness.

Plan your approach and tone. Decide how you want to start the conversation, what language you&#x27;ll use, and how you&#x27;ll maintain a calm and professional demeanor. Consider using &quot;I&quot; statements to express your feelings and avoid blaming or accusatory language.

Practice active listening by paying attention to the other person&#x27;s words, tone, and body language. This can help you respond thoughtfully and avoid escalating the situation.

Consider the timing and setting for the conversation. Choose a private, quiet space where you both feel comfortable and won&#x27;t be interrupted. Make sure you both have enough time to discuss the issue without feeling rushed.

Lastly, be prepared for different outcomes. The conversation may not go exactly as planned, and that&#x27;s okay. Stay flexible, and be willing to adjust your approach if needed.

By taking the time to prepare, you can feel more confident and in control during the conversation. Remember, the goal is to resolve the issue and improve the working relationship, not to &quot;win&quot; an argument.
</pre>
