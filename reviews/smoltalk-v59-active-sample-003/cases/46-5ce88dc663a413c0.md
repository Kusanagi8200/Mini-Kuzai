### Turn 4 - ASSISTANT

<pre>
Approaching your supervisor can be intimidating, but being honest and prepared is key. Before the conversation, take some time to reflect on your specific challenges and what you hope to achieve from the discussion. Consider writing down your thoughts and concerns to help you stay focused.

When you meet with your supervisor, start by expressing your enthusiasm for the role and the company, and then explain how you&#x27;re feeling overwhelmed due to the lack of experience and training. Be specific about the areas where you&#x27;re struggling and the mistakes you&#x27;ve made. Avoid blaming others or making excuses; instead, focus on your own experiences and what you need to succeed.

You might say something like, &quot;I&#x27;m really excited to be working in this role, but I&#x27;m finding it challenging to get up to speed without formal training or guidance. I&#x27;ve been trying to figure things out on my own, but I&#x27;ve made a few mistakes, and I&#x27;m worried that I&#x27;m not meeting expectations. I was hoping we could discuss possible ways to get me the support and training I need to succeed in this role.&quot;

Be clear about what you&#x27;re asking for - whether it&#x27;s additional training, regular check-ins, or guidance from a more experienced colleague. Show that you&#x27;re proactive and committed to learning by suggesting potential solutions or resources that you&#x27;ve researched.

For example, you could say, &quot;I&#x27;ve been looking into online courses that could help me develop my skills in [specific area]. Would it be possible to discuss reimbursement for these courses or allocation of time to complete them during work hours?&quot;

Remember to listen to your supervisor&#x27;s response and be open to their suggestions and feedback. They may have valuable insights or solutions that you haven&#x27;t considered.

### Turn 6 - ASSISTANT

<pre>
If your supervisor responds that there isn&#x27;t money for training and suggests finding free resources, try not to get discouraged. This response doesn&#x27;t necessarily mean that they&#x27;re not invested in your success, but rather that the company may be facing budget constraints.

You could respond by acknowledging the budget constraints and expressing your understanding. Then, ask if there are any internal resources or colleagues who could provide guidance or mentorship. This shows that you&#x27;re willing to work within the existing constraints to find a solution.

For example, you could say, &quot;I understand that budget can be a challenge. Some companies may not be able to provide formal training, but they may be willing to support your attendance at industry events or conferences.

If your supervisor is still unable to provide any support, you may need to take matters into your own hands. You can start by searching for free online resources, such as blogs, podcasts, or online communities related to your field. You could also reach out to your professional network or attend local industry events to connect with others who may be able to offer guidance or advice.

It&#x27;s also important to consider whether this lack of support is a long-term concern for you. If you feel that you&#x27;re not getting the support you need to succeed in your role, it may be worth exploring other job opportunities where professional development is a priority.
