To determine significant differences between groups in a dataset, the best approach often involves using statistical tests, such as hypothesis testing or confidence intervals. The specific method depends on the research question, data type, and distribution.

### Turn 6 - ASSISTANT

<pre>
There are several ways to determine if data is normally distributed. Visual inspection is a good starting point, using plots like histograms, density plots, or Q-Q plots (quantile-quantile plots). These plots can help you visually assess whether the data follows a normal distribution, characterized by a bell-shaped curve.

In a histogram or density plot, look for a symmetrical distribution with a peak in the middle and tapering off towards the extremes. A Q-Q plot compares the data to a normal distribution; if the data points lie close to the reference line, it suggests normality.

Statistical methods can also be used to test for normality, such as the Shapiro-Wilk test or the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. These tests provide a p-value indicating the probability that the data is normally distributed. A p-value greater than a certain significance level (e.g., 0.05) suggests that the data is likely normally distributed.

Another approach is to check for skewness and kurtosis, which measure the data&#x27;s symmetry and tail heaviness, respectively. If these values are close to zero, it indicates a normal distribution.

It&#x27;s essential to note that no real-world data is perfectly normally distributed. However, many statistical tests are robust to minor deviations from normality. The key is to determine whether the data is &quot;normal enough&quot; to satisfy the assumptions of the test you&#x27;re using.
</pre>
