It&#x27;s completely understandable that you&#x27;re torn about this decision, especially considering the potential impact on your future career prospects. On one hand, taking the job offer from your friend could provide you with a much-needed steady income and valuable work experience, which is crucial at this point in your life. On the other hand, your concerns about the low salary and potential long-term effects on your career advancement are valid. It&#x27;s essential to consider whether this role will give you the skills and experience you need to grow professionally and increase your earning potential.

It&#x27;s also essential to discuss your concerns with your friend and see if there&#x27;s room for salary growth or professional development within the company.

Ultimately, it&#x27;s crucial to weigh your immediate needs against your long-term goals and consider what&#x27;s best for you and your family. ### Turn 4 - ASSISTANT

<pre>
Discussing salary with a friend who&#x27;s also your employer can be delicate, but it&#x27;s essential to approach the conversation in a professional and respectful manner. After you join the company, consider waiting for a few months to get a feel for the work, understand the company&#x27;s expectations, and demonstrate your value to the business.

When you&#x27;re ready to discuss salary, schedule a meeting with your friend to review your performance and discuss your future with the company. Prepare for the conversation by making a list of your achievements, the skills you&#x27;ve developed, and the value you&#x27;ve added to the business. This will help you make a strong case for why you deserve a salary increase.

You might start the conversation by expressing your gratitude for the opportunity to work with your friend and acknowledging the initial salary agreement. Then, explain that you&#x27;ve been reflecting on your compensation and would like to discuss the possibility of a salary adjustment. Be specific about what you&#x27;re hoping to achieve, whether it&#x27;s a specific salary range or a performance-based raise.

It&#x27;s essential to focus on your professional growth, industry standards, and the value you bring to the company, rather than making a personal appeal to your friend. You might say something like, &quot;I&#x27;ve been doing some research, and I believe my salary is below the industry average for someone with my experience and qualifications. Considering my contributions to the company, I was hoping we could discuss the possibility of a salary increase to bring my compensation more in line with industry standards.&quot;

By framing the conversation around your professional growth and the value you bring to the company, you can help your friend see the discussion as a business decision rather than a personal favor. Be prepared to negotiate and find a mutually agreeable solution that works for both you and the company.
</pre>

### Turn 6 - ASSISTANT

<pre>
As you start your new job, it&#x27;s essential to pay attention to signs that may indicate the role isn&#x27;t a good fit for you. Here are some warning signs to watch out for:

If you find that your responsibilities are consistently mismatched with your skills, interests, or expectations, it may be a sign that the job isn&#x27;t a good fit. Perhaps the work is too repetitive, too stressful, or too mundane, leading to feelings of boredom, frustration, or burnout.

Another warning sign is if you&#x27;re having trouble communicating effectively with your friend and employer, or if you feel like your concerns or ideas are being dismissed. As a friend, your employer may unintentionally blur the lines between your personal and professional relationship, making it challenging to address issues or set boundaries.

Pay attention to the company culture and work environment. If you find that the values, norms, or expectations of the company clash with your own, it may create tension and make it difficult for you to thrive. For example, if the company prioritizes long working hours, but you need to balance work with family responsibilities, it may not be a sustainable fit.

Additionally, if you&#x27;re not receiving the support, training, or resources you need to succeed in your role, it may indicate that the company is not invested in your growth or well-being. This could be a sign that the job is not a good fit, or that the company is not a healthy or sustainable work environment.

Lastly, trust your instincts and pay attention to your overall job satisfaction and happiness. If you consistently feel unhappy, unfulfilled, or stressed, it may be time to reassess whether this job is truly a stepping stone or a dead-end.

Remember, it&#x27;s okay to acknowledge that a job isn&#x27;t working out and to start exploring other options. Your goal is to find a career path that aligns with your values, skills, and aspirations, and sometimes that means taking calculated risks and making changes.
</pre>
