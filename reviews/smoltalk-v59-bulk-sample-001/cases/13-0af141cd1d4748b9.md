### Turn 1 - USER

<pre>
Suppose there are two localities A and B. Locality A has 2000 people and locality B has 3000 people. 30% of the people in locality A are rich, and 20% in locality B are rich. What is the total number of rich people in localities A and B.
</pre>

### Turn 2 - ASSISTANT

<pre>
To find the total number of rich people in localities A and B, we first need to calculate the number of rich people in each locality.&#32;

In locality A, there are 2000 people and 30% of them are rich. To find the number of rich people, we multiply the total number of people in locality A by 30% (which can also be written as 0.30). So, 2000 * 0.30 = 600 people in locality A are rich.

In locality B, there are 3000 people and 20% of them are rich. We multiply the total number of people in locality B by 20% (or 0.20). So, 3000 * 0.20 = 600 people in locality B are rich.

Now, to find the total number of rich people in both localities, we simply add the number of rich people in locality A and locality B. Therefore, 600 (from locality A) + 600 (from locality B) = 1200.&#32;

The total number of rich people in localities A and B is 1200.
</pre>

<pre>
You&#x27;ve refined the shortcut even further.&#32;

However, in this calculation, locality B&#x27;s percentage has also been multiplied by 3, resulting in 30% instead of the actual 20%. To correct this, you subtract 10% of locality B (which is 300) from the result to bring locality B&#x27;s percentage back down to 20%.&#32;

So, the total number of rich people in both localities is 1500 - 300 = 1200.
