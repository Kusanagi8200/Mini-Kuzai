### Turn 2 - ASSISTANT

<pre>
The reason why assigning a value to the subproperty in a map results in an error while assigning to the subproperty in an array works fine is because map entries are not addressable. This means that you cannot take the address of an entry in a map. The language does not allow assigning to a specific field of a struct in a map because adding a new value to a map may cause map entries to shift around, resulting in changing addresses. Therefore, all map operations use whole values: copying a whole value out of a map or adding a whole value to a map.

On the other hand, elements in arrays and slices are addressable because they do not move around after they have been created. This allows you to assign a value to a specific field of a struct in an array without any issues.

As for why the language has this difference between maps and arrays, it is likely due to the trade-offs and performance considerations. Allowing read-modify-write operations on map entries would introduce additional complexity and potential performance overhead. By restricting map operations to whole values, the language can optimize map operations for efficiency.
</pre>
