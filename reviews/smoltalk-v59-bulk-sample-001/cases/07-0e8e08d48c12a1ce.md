### Turn 2 - ASSISTANT

<pre>
Passive voice is a grammatical construction where the focus is on the action&#x27;s recipient or the action itself, rather than the doer of the action. This can make the sentence seem less direct or less engaging, as it often omits or downplays the role of the actor.

In a passive voice construction, the subject of the sentence is typically the recipient of the action, and the verb is often in a form that includes a form of &quot;to be&quot; (such as &quot;is,&quot; &quot;was,&quot; or &quot;were&quot;) followed by the past participle of the main verb. For example:

Passive: &quot;The ball was thrown by John.&quot;
Active: &quot;John threw the ball.&quot;

In the passive version, the focus is on the ball (the recipient of the action), while in the active version, the focus is on John (the doer of the action).

Using passive voice is not always a bad thing; it can be useful in certain situations, such as when the doer of the action is unknown or unimportant, or when you want to emphasize the action itself. However, excessive use of passive voice can make your writing seem vague, wordy, or less engaging.

To improve clarity and directness, it&#x27;s often a good idea to rephrase passive voice constructions into active voice, especially in sentences where the doer of the action is important or relevant to the context.
</pre>

<pre>
Passive voice is when you focus on what&#x27;s happening to something, rather than who&#x27;s doing it. This can make your writing less clear and less interesting.

For example, &quot;The ball was thrown&quot; is passive because it focuses on the ball, not on who threw it. A more direct way to say it is &quot;John threw the ball,&quot; which focuses on the person doing the action.

It&#x27;s not always wrong to use passive voice, but using it too much can make your writing seem unclear or boring. Changing passive sentences to active ones can make your writing more direct and engaging.
</pre>

In this case, using passive voice is acceptable because the focus is on the fact that the policy has been implemented, rather than who specifically implemented it. The doer of the action (the person or team that implemented the policy) is not important to the message, so omitting them doesn&#x27;t make the sentence unclear or misleading.

In fact, using passive voice in this case makes the sentence more concise and to the point, as it avoids unnecessary detail. Additionally, using active voice in this case could even make the sentence seem awkward or overly focused on the wrong information, such as: &quot;The management team implemented the new policy.&quot;

So, in situations where the doer of the action is unknown, unimportant, or obvious from the context, using passive voice can be a good choice.
