The sentence &quot;I&#x27;m going to the store, can you tell me if they have milk?&quot; is mostly clear in its intention, but it&#x27;s technically a comma splice, which is a grammatical error. A comma splice occurs when two independent clauses (i.e., clauses that could stand alone as separate sentences) are joined with a comma without proper conjunction.

A revised version of the sentence could be:

&quot;I&#x27;m going to the store. Can you tell me if they have milk?&quot;

This breaks the sentence into two separate sentences, making it grammatically correct. Alternatively, you could use a conjunction to link the two clauses:

&quot;I&#x27;m going to the store; can you tell me if they have milk?&quot;

Or:

&quot;I&#x27;m going to the store, so can you tell me if they have milk?&quot;

These revised versions are grammatically correct and convey the same meaning as the original sentence.

This change puts the pronouns in the correct order and uses the correct subject pronoun &quot;I.&quot; It&#x27;s also considered more polite to list the other person first, which is why &quot;my sister&quot; comes before &quot;I&quot; in the revised sentence.
