### Turn 6 - ASSISTANT

<pre>
A comma splice is actually an error in punctuation, not a deliberate grammatical function. It occurs when two independent clauses (i.e., complete sentences with a subject and verb) are joined with a comma without proper conjunction or punctuation. This can lead to confusion and make the text harder to read.

Comma splices are indeed common in writing, often due to a writer&#x27;s attempt to link closely related ideas together. However, to fix a comma splice, you can use one of several methods: separate the clauses into distinct sentences, add a conjunction (like &quot;and,&quot; &quot;but,&quot; or &quot;or&quot;) after the comma, or use a semicolon to indicate a stronger break between the clauses. By correcting comma splices, you can improve the clarity and overall flow of your writing.
</pre>
