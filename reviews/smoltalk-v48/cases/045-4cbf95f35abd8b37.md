### Turn 1 - USER

<pre>
I am writing a story that has this sentence in it. &quot;The new policy effects all employees starting next month.&quot; Should I use &quot;effects&quot; or &quot;affects&quot;?
</pre>

### Turn 2 - ASSISTANT

<pre>
In this case, you should use &quot;affects&quot; instead of &quot;effects.&quot; The verb &quot;affects&quot; means to influence or have an impact on something, whereas &quot;effects&quot; is a noun that refers to the results or consequences of a particular action.

So, the corrected sentence would be: &quot;The new policy affects all employees starting next month.&quot; This indicates that the policy will have an impact on or influence all employees.

If you were to use &quot;effects&quot; in this sentence, you would be saying that the policy is producing results or consequences for the employees, which is not the intended meaning. For example, a correct usage of &quot;effects&quot; might be: &quot;The effects of the new policy on employee morale are still unclear.&quot;
</pre>

### Turn 3 - USER

<pre>
What is the best way to use active voice in that sentence instead of passive voice?&#32;

Instead of, &quot;The new policy affects all employees starting next month,&quot; is &quot;The new policy will affect all employees starting next month,&quot; better.
</pre>

### Turn 4 - ASSISTANT

<pre>
First, you&#x27;re correct that adding &quot;will affect&quot; makes the sentence slightly more active and clear by indicating a future action. However, the sentence is still somewhat passive because the focus is on the policy rather than the entity performing the action (the company or management).

To make the sentence more active, you could rephrase it to emphasize the entity implementing the policy. For example:

&quot;The company will implement a new policy affecting all employees starting next month.&quot;

Or:

&quot;Management will introduce a new policy that affects all employees starting next month.&quot;

In both of these revised sentences, the focus is on the entity performing the action (the company or management), making the sentence more active and engaging. The original sentence, while clear, is more passive because it focuses on the policy itself rather than the entity driving the action.
</pre>

(2) &quot;Sarah shot Emily a quick look of surprise before responding.&quot;

(3) &quot;Sarah and Emily exchanged a fleeting glance, and then Sarah responded.&quot;

Which sentence best captures the moment in my story between the two characters?
</pre>

### Turn 6 - ASSISTANT

<pre>
All three options convey a brief, meaningful exchange between Sarah and Emily, but they have slightly different connotations.

Option (1) &quot;Sarah exchanged a quick look of surprise with Emily before responding&quot; implies a more mutual and deliberate exchange of glances. The word &quot;exchanged&quot; suggests that both characters are actively participating in the moment, and the look is a two-way communication.

Option (2) &quot;Sarah shot Emily a quick look of surprise before responding&quot; is more one-sided. The word &quot;shot&quot; implies a rapid, almost instinctive glance from Sarah to Emily, which might not necessarily be reciprocated. This sentence puts more emphasis on Sarah&#x27;s action and reaction.

Option (3) &quot;Sarah and Emily exchanged a fleeting glance, and then Sarah responded&quot; is similar to option (1) but uses more descriptive language. The phrase &quot;fleeting glance&quot; creates a sense of brevity and subtlety, implying that the exchange is brief and perhaps not even consciously acknowledged by the characters.

Considering your options, if you want to convey a sense of mutual understanding or shared surprise between Sarah and Emily, options (1) or (3) might be a better choice. If you want to emphasize Sarah&#x27;s reaction and initiative, option (2) could be more suitable.

However, if I had to pick one sentence that best captures the moment, I&#x27;d suggest option (3) &quot;Sarah and Emily exchanged a fleeting glance, and then Sarah responded.&quot; This sentence creates a vivid image of a brief, wordless exchange between the two characters, and the phrase &quot;fleeting glance&quot; adds a touch of nuance to the scene.
</pre>
