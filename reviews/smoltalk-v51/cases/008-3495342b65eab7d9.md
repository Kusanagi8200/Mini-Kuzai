### Turn 6 - ASSISTANT

<pre>
Person B is committing the logical fallacy of &quot;Argument from Ignorance&quot; or &quot;Absence of Evidence is Evidence of Absence.&quot; This fallacy occurs when someone assumes that the lack of evidence or observation of something is proof that it does not exist.

In this case, Person B is arguing that because they cannot see God, God must not exist. However, the absence of visual evidence does not necessarily prove that God does not exist. There may be many things that exist but are not visible or directly observable, such as emotions, thoughts, or abstract concepts.

Furthermore, Person B&#x27;s argument relies on the assumption that visibility is the only means of determining existence, which is a narrow and limited perspective. There may be other ways to experience or understand God&#x27;s existence, such as through spiritual experiences, philosophical reasoning, or faith.

To demonstrate the flaw in Person B&#x27;s argument, consider the following counterexample: &quot;I don&#x27;t see gravity, therefore, gravity does not exist.&quot; Just because we cannot see gravity does not mean it does not exist. We can still observe its effects and infer its presence through other means.

Person B&#x27;s argument is an example of a logical fallacy because it assumes a false dichotomy between visibility and existence, and it fails to consider alternative explanations or perspectives.
</pre>
