### Turn 2 - ASSISTANT

<pre>
This is a classic problem of combinations, where the order of the selection doesn&#x27;t matter. To solve this, we can use the combination formula, which is nCr = n! / (r!(n-r)!), where n is the total number of items and r is the number of items being chosen.

In this case, we have 6 balls and we want to choose 3 of them. So, n = 6 and r = 3. Plugging these values into the formula, we get:

6C3 = 6! / (3!(6-3)!)
= 6! / (3! * 3!)
= (6*5*4) / (3*2*1)
= 20

So, there are 20 different groups of 3 balls that you can pick out of the 6 total balls. This makes sense, since the order of the selection doesn&#x27;t matter, and we&#x27;re only concerned with the unique combinations of 3 balls.
</pre>
