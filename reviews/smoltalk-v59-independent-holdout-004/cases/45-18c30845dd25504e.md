### Turn 2 - ASSISTANT

<pre>
With the first term (a) being 12 and the common difference (d) being 4, we have the foundation for an arithmetic sequence. An arithmetic sequence is a sequence of numbers in which each term after the first is obtained by adding a fixed constant to the previous term. In this case, starting with 12, we would add 4 to get the next term, and continue adding 4 to each subsequent term.&#32;

So, our sequence would look like 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, and so on. The nth term of an arithmetic sequence can also be calculated directly using the formula: an = a + (n-1)d, where an is the nth term, a is the first term, n is the term number, and d is the common difference. This setup provides an efficient way to find any term in the sequence without needing to list out all the preceding terms.
</pre>

Here, starting with the first term (a) of 2 and a common ratio (r) of 3, our geometric sequence would look like 2, 6, 18, 54, 162, and so on. Each term is obtained by multiplying the preceding term by 3.

The nth term of a geometric sequence can be calculated directly using the formula: an = ar^(n-1), where an is the nth term, a is the first term, r is the common ratio, and n is the term number. This formula allows us to efficiently find any term in the sequence.
