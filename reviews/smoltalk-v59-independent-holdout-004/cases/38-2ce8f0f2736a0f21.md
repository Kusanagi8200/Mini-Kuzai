Precise writing is crucial in academic writing as it enables authors to convey complex ideas and arguments clearly and effectively. Some key features of precise writing include:

Using specific and concise language to avoid ambiguity and confusion. This involves choosing words that accurately convey the intended meaning and avoiding vague or overly broad terms.

Employing active voice instead of passive voice, which can often make sentences seem less direct and less clear.

Avoiding jargon and technical terms unless they are necessary for the subject matter and have been clearly defined.

Are there any words or phrases that could be ambiguous or confusing to the reader?

What is meant by passive voice in the context of the written word? In written passages, how does one determine if a sentence is in passive voice?
</pre>

### Turn 4 - ASSISTANT

<pre>
In the context of written language, passive voice refers to a grammatical construction where the subject of a sentence receives the action described by the verb, rather than performing it. This is in contrast to active voice, where the subject of the sentence performs the action described by the verb.

To determine if a sentence is in passive voice, look for the following characteristics:

The subject of the sentence is not the one performing the action. Instead, the subject is the recipient of the action.

The verb is often accompanied by a form of the verb &quot;to be&quot; (such as &quot;was,&quot; &quot;is,&quot; &quot;were,&quot; or &quot;been&quot;).

The sentence may include a phrase beginning with &quot;by&quot; that indicates the performer of the action.

Here&#x27;s an example of a sentence in passive voice:

&quot;The new policy was implemented by the management team last quarter.&quot;

In this sentence:

The subject is &quot;the new policy,&quot; which receives the action of being implemented.

The verb &quot;was implemented&quot; is in a passive form, accompanied by the verb &quot;was.&quot;

The phrase &quot;by the management team&quot; indicates the performer of the action.

To rewrite this sentence in active voice, you could say:

&quot;The management team implemented the new policy last quarter.&quot;

In this revised sentence, the subject &quot;the management team&quot; performs the action of implementing the policy, making the sentence active.

It&#x27;s worth noting that not all sentences with forms of &quot;to be&quot; are passive. For example, the sentence &quot;She is happy&quot; is not passive, because &quot;is happy&quot; describes a state of being, rather than an action being performed on the subject.

Pay attention to the relationships between the subject, verb, and any additional phrases to help you identify and revise passive voice in your writing.
</pre>

To identify sentences in written passages that need to be changed from passive to active voice, follow these steps:

Read the passage carefully, paying attention to the subject-verb relationships in each sentence.

Look for sentences where the subject is not performing the action described by the verb. Instead, the subject is receiving the action or is having something done to it.

Check for verbs that are in a passive form, often accompanied by a form of the verb &quot;to be&quot; (such as &quot;was,&quot; &quot;is,&quot; &quot;were,&quot; or &quot;been&quot;).

Identify phrases beginning with &quot;by&quot; that indicate the performer of the action. These phrases can be a good indicator of passive voice.

Consider the clarity and directness of the sentence. If the sentence seems wordy or unclear, it may be a good candidate for revision from passive to active voice.

Ask yourself questions like:

Who is performing the action in this sentence?

Is the subject of the sentence clear and direct?

Can the sentence be rephrased to make it more concise and clear?

Some common phrases that may indicate passive voice include:

&quot;was [verb] by&quot;
&quot;is [verb] by&quot;
&quot;were [verb] by&quot;
&quot;has been [verb] by&quot;
&quot;had been [verb] by&quot;

Examples of sentences that may need to be changed from passive to active voice:

&quot;The research was conducted by a team of scientists.&quot;

&quot;The new policy has been implemented by the management team.&quot;

&quot;The play was written by a famous playwright.&quot;

These sentences can be revised to active voice by making the performer of the action the subject of the sentence:

&quot;A team of scientists conducted the research.&quot;

&quot;The management team implemented the new policy.&quot;

&quot;A famous playwright wrote the play.&quot;

By revising sentences from passive to active voice, you can make your writing more clear, concise, and engaging.
