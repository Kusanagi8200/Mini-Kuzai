### Turn 2 - ASSISTANT

<pre>
The Beltrami-Klein model is a representation of the hyperbolic plane within a Euclidean disk, where geodesics (shortest paths) are realized as straight line segments, but distances and angles are not preserved in a straightforward manner as in Euclidean geometry. What sets it apart from the Poincaré models, such as the Poincaré disk model and the Poincaré half-plane model, is its unique representation of angles.&#32;

In the Beltrami-Klein model, angles are not preserved, meaning that the angle between two geodesics intersecting at a point in the hyperbolic plane does not equal the Euclidean angle between the corresponding straight line segments at the corresponding point in the model. In contrast, both the Poincaré disk and half-plane models are conformal, meaning that angles in these models are preserved: the angle between two geodesics in the model equals the true angle between the corresponding hyperbolic geodesics.

However, the Beltrami-Klein model&#x27;s representation of geodesics as straight line segments simplifies certain calculations and considerations, particularly those involving the projective and incidence properties of hyperbolic geometry, making it a valuable tool for studying various aspects of hyperbolic geometry.
