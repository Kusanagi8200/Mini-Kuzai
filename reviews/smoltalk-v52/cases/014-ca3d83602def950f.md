When conveying technical skills in a non-technical resume for data analysis work, the goal is to showcase your expertise without overwhelming the reader with technical jargon. Here are some tips to help you achieve this balance:

Instead of listing specific tools or programming languages, focus on the skills and abilities they enable. For example, instead of saying &quot;Proficient in SQL,&quot; say &quot;Adept at extracting insights from large datasets using database querying techniques.&quot; This approach highlights your ability to work with data without requiring the reader to be familiar with the specific tool.

Use functional language to describe your technical skills. For instance, &quot;Data visualization and reporting,&quot; &quot;Statistical modeling and analysis,&quot; or &quot;Data mining and predictive analytics&quot; are phrases that convey technical expertise without being too specific.

Emphasize the benefits of your technical skills, such as &quot;Improved data-driven decision-making,&quot; &quot;Enhanced business intelligence,&quot; or &quot;Optimized process efficiency.&quot; This shows how your technical abilities can drive business outcomes and adds context to your skills.

If you need to mention specific tools or software, consider grouping them together under a broad category, such as &quot;Data analysis software (e.g., Excel, Tableau, Power BI)&quot; or &quot;Programming languages (e.g., Python, R, SQL).&quot; This approach gives a sense of your technical range without overwhelming the reader.

Lastly, be concise and selective in highlighting your technical skills. Choose the most relevant and impactful ones that align with the job requirements, and avoid listing every tool or technology you&#x27;ve ever worked with.
